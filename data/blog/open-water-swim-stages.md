If you've only swum in a pool, your first open-water swim can come as a surprise. Nobody warns you about the lane lines that aren't there. Here are the five stages most people pass through, in order.

## 1. Confidence

You've done plenty of laps. The water looks nice. You're ready.

## 2. The cold

The first thirty seconds feel like someone has been emptying a freezer into the lake. Your breathing goes strange. This is normal, and it settles. Wade in, splash your face and give yourself a minute before you start swimming.

## 3. The missing line

In a pool, there is a black line on the bottom telling you exactly where to go. In open water there is nothing. You'll find you've been swimming sideways for a while. The fix is called **sighting**: every few strokes, lift your eyes forward just enough to spot a landmark (a buoy, a tree, a roof) and then put your face back down.

## 4. The panic

At some point, usually about three minutes in, you will feel the urge to stop. It's not a sign that something is wrong. It's a mix of cold water, excited breathing and an unfamiliar environment. What helps:

- Roll onto your back and float for a moment
- Breathe out fully underwater, even if it feels odd
- Swim breaststroke for a few strokes until your breathing settles
- Hold on to a buoy or paddleboard if there is one

Then keep going. Nobody is timing you.

## 5. The grin

Somewhere in the second half, it clicks. The water feels easy, your stroke gets longer, and you realize you are swimming across a lake. You'll climb out looking pleased with yourself, and you'll be allowed.

## A few practical tips

1. **Never swim alone.** Bring a friend, or better, a group.
2. **Wear a bright cap** so others can see you.
3. **Practice getting in.** Wade in up to your waist and swim a short loop near the shore before heading out.
4. **Start at the side or back of the pack** at a race, and give yourself space.
5. **Check conditions first.** Cold, current and water quality all matter.

Pools are great, but at some point the lake becomes a favorite.
