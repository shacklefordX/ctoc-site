At some point, every triathlete ends up asking the same thing: do I really need a tri suit? Short answer: not for your first race, but you may well want one after that. Here's what you're getting.

## What is a tri suit?

A tri suit is a single outfit made to be worn for all three sports. It's snug, quick-drying and has a thin pad, so it works in the water, on the bike and on the run without a change of clothes. You wear it from the start of the swim to the finish line.

## One piece or two?

- **One-piece suits** are the classic. There's nothing to ride up or flap around, and it's the quickest to put on.
- **Two-piece (top and shorts)** are easier for bathroom stops and for fitting different sizes top and bottom. Many people prefer them for longer races.
- **Sleeved or sleeveless?** Sleeves give some sun cover and cut a little wind. Sleeveless feels cooler and gives your shoulders room to swim.

## What to look for

- **A thin pad.** It needs to be comfortable on the bike but not so thick that it soaks up water or feels bulky when you run. It won't feel like a cycling short, and that's by design.
- **Fit.** Snug, but not restrictive. You should be able to take a full breath, reach forward and sit on a bike comfortably. Avoid anything that digs in.
- **Pockets.** Handy for gels on a long ride. Not essential for a sprint.
- **Fabric.** It should dry quickly and feel smooth against your skin.
- **Sizing.** Sizes differ between brands, so check each brand's chart and go by measurements rather than your usual size.

## Test it before race day

This is the one thing everyone wishes they'd known earlier. Wear the suit for a swim, a ride and a run before the race. Look for places that rub, and use an anti-chafe balm where it matters. Race day is the worst time to learn that your new suit doesn't fit.

## What if you don't want to buy one yet?

Totally fine. For a first sprint, a swimsuit or tri-style shorts and a top you already own works. Wear whatever is comfortable and won't weigh you down once it's wet.

## Wetsuits and tri suits

If you end up racing in a wetsuit, the tri suit goes underneath it. That's one more reason to have one, since there's nothing to take off when you get out of the water.
