Walk into a bike shop or scroll through a triathlon website and it can feel like you need a second mortgage before you're allowed to race. You don't. Here's what actually matters for a first triathlon, in the order I'd buy it.

## The golden rule

**Use what you have first.** Do a few sessions with the bike, shoes and swimsuit already in your closet. After a month, you'll know exactly what is annoying you, and that's what to replace.

## Buy first

### 1. Goggles that fit

This is the cheapest upgrade with the biggest effect. Leaky or foggy goggles ruin a swim. Try a few pairs on at a shop, press them to your eyes without the strap, and see if they stick for a second. If they do, they probably fit. Bring a spare pair on race day.

### 2. A helmet

You'll need one for every ride and every race, and it should fit snugly and sit level on your head. It's the one thing worth paying a bit more for. Replace it after any crash.

### 3. Running shoes that suit your feet

Get fitted at a running store if you can. Comfort matters more than the brand or the price. If your current pair is worn out, start here.

### 4. A bike that works

Any road, hybrid or mountain bike that is mechanically sound can get you through a sprint. A tune-up at a local shop (brakes, gears, tires) is better money than a new bike. Make sure the tires are properly inflated before every ride.

## Buy next

- **A tri suit or tri shorts.** One outfit for all three sports means no changing in the middle, and it dries quickly. It's comfortable on the bike and doesn't rub on the run. You can also wear it for training.
- **Basic bike repair kit.** A spare tube, tire levers and a pump or CO2 inflator. Learn to change a tube at home before you need to do it on the side of the road.
- **Race belt.** It holds your number so you don't have to pin it to your shirt.
- **Water bottles and a cage.** If you're riding for more than an hour, you need to drink.

## Maybe, someday

- **A wetsuit.** Useful for cold open water, but you don't need to own one for a first race. Some races let you rent one.
- **Clip-in pedals and shoes.** They make riding more efficient, but they take practice. Many riders try them after a few months.
- **A bike computer or watch.** Nice to have for pacing. It won't make you faster on day one.
- **Aero bars.** Make you faster in a straight line and a bit harder to steer in a group. Not for the first race.

## What you can skip

- A triathlon-specific bike
- Carbon wheels
- A power meter
- Anything a salesperson says you'll "regret not having"

## A simple first-race kit

1. Swimsuit or tri suit
2. Goggles (and a spare)
3. Helmet
4. Bike in working order
5. Running shoes
6. Race belt
7. Water bottle
8. Sunglasses and sunscreen

That's it. Everyone else in the transition area has more gear than you, and on the course nobody cares.

If you aren't sure whether something is worth buying, ask on a group ride. Most of us own at least one thing that was a waste of money, and we'll happily tell you which.
