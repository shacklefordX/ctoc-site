Walk up to a group of triathletes and you'll hear a language that sounds like a mix of gym class, a bike shop and an airline. Here's your translator, so nobody has to nod along while quietly panicking.

## The places

- **T1 and T2.** Transition one (swim to bike) and transition two (bike to run). Two small patches of ground where you rack your bike and change gear. Your clock keeps running, which is why you'll hear people brag about fast transitions.
- **The transition area.** Rows of bikes on racks, with each athlete's gear on a little towel. The most organized place on race morning, and also the most nervous.

## The workouts

- **Brick.** A bike ride followed straight away by a run. Named because your legs feel like bricks for the first few minutes. They get better with practice, and everyone has the same strange first steps.
- **Zone 2.** An easy pace where you can still hold a conversation. It's the pace most of your training should be at, and also the one new athletes find hardest to hold back to.
- **Long run / long ride.** Whatever your longest session of the week is. For us, that's Saturday's ride and Sunday's run.

## The gear

- **Aero.** Making yourself as slippery as possible on the bike. Sometimes this means expensive equipment, and sometimes it just means tucking your elbows.
- **Wetsuit-legal.** A race where the water is cool enough that wetsuits are allowed. The word you'll hear after "is it" before every spring swim.
- **Tri kit.** A one- or two-piece suit you wear for all three sports, so you don't have to change in the middle. Some people also train in one, and you can wear it to a group ride.

## The feelings

- **Bonk.** Running out of fuel mid-session. It ends with a sudden need for a banana. The fix is to eat and drink before you need to.
- **PR (or PB).** Personal record. A new best time. Anyone will tell you theirs without being asked.
- **DNF.** "Did not finish." It happens to everybody eventually, and nobody holds it against you.
- **Chicked.** Passed by someone you weren't expecting. Usually said with respect.

## The golden rule

When someone says something you don't understand, ask. Every triathlete in the club was a beginner once, and most of them still remember how confusing it was.
