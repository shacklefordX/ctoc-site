Every triathlon community has a couple of races that everybody ends up talking about. In the Treasure Valley, it's two: a flat, fast sprint along the river that's perfect for a first race, and a half-Ironman that starts in a reservoir and finishes downtown. They're seven weeks apart in 2027, and they make a very good pair.

*Details below were checked in October 2026. Dates and distances can change, so confirm on each race's own site before you sign up.*

## Race one: Treasure Valley Triathlon

**Saturday, June 5, 2027 · Esther Simplot Park, Boise**

If you've been wondering whether triathlon is for you, this is your race.

The course is a **circuit-style triathlon on a closed course** along the Boise River. That means you loop back through familiar ground instead of heading off down the road, so there's no traffic to worry about, and the people who came to cheer for you can stay in one spot and see you again and again. The organizers call it flat, fast and good for racers of all levels, and it shows in the menu:

- **Super Sprint:** the gentlest way in
- **Sprint:** the classic first race
- **Olympic:** for when a sprint starts to feel easy
- **Relays:** swim, bike or run just one leg, and bring two friends for the others
- **Youth Splash and Dash:** because kids deserve a finish line too
- **Adaptive categories:** the race is also part of the USAT Para National Qualifier Series

It's USAT sanctioned, and in 2027 it doubles as the **USAT Idaho State Championship**. You don't need to care about that to enjoy it, but it does mean you could be racing alongside some very fast people. (Don't worry, you won't see them again after the swim.)

The swim, bike and run distances are on the race's own course pages, so look there before you plan your training.

[Race page and registration](https://runsignup.com/Race/ID/Boise/TreasureValleyTriathlon)

## Race two: IRONMAN 70.3 Boise

**Saturday, July 24, 2027 · Lucky Peak to downtown Boise**

This is the one people build a year around. A 70.3 is **1.2 miles of swimming, 56 miles of biking and 13.1 miles of running**, 70.3 miles in total, and the Boise course has a lot going for it.

- **The swim** starts and finishes at **Barclay Bay in Lucky Peak Reservoir**. A reservoir swim at dawn is a very good way to wake up.
- **The bike** begins with a **long, sweeping downhill** out of the reservoir area, then runs through rural roads and neighborhood streets and brings you into downtown. Save your legs on that opening descent. You'll want them later.
- **The run** is **two loops of the Boise River Greenbelt**, with shade, spectators and the kind of familiar path many of us run on a Tuesday. Today it's a race course.
- **The finish** is downtown. In 2026 it was a festival at Julia Davis Park with food, music and vendors.

IRONMAN came back to Boise in 2025, and for local athletes that's the best part. Instead of traveling to a race, you get to race the roads and paths you train on. In 2025 the race ran on July 26 and in 2026 on July 25, so late July is clearly where it has landed.

It's also a big step. Most people take months to build up to a half distance, which is why it's better as a second or third season goal than a first one.

[Official race page](https://www.ironman.com/races/im703-boise)

## Doing both

The two races are 49 days apart, which makes the first a natural tune-up for the second.

- **Now to March:** build a base. Easy swimming, riding and running, lots of Zone 2, and a few Saturday group rides to get the miles in.
- **April to early June:** the build. This is when the [8-week sprint plan](/training-plans) fits, working back from June 5.
- **June 5:** Treasure Valley Triathlon. Race it hard or race it as practice. Either way you'll learn how your nutrition, transitions and nerves hold up.
- **June 6 to July 24:** a final seven-week build, with long rides and a taper in the last stretch.
- **July 24:** IRONMAN 70.3 Boise.

If you're not ready for the second one, that's fine. Do the first, then cheer on the second. Lining a course is a good way to find out whether you want to be on it.
