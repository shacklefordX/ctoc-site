Triathlon in the Treasure Valley has two big names. One is a friendly, flat sprint on the river that makes a great first race. The other is a half-Ironman that people train for all year. Here's how they compare, so you can pick one (or both, in different years).

*Details below were checked in October 2026. Dates and distances can change, so confirm on each race's own site before you sign up.*

## Treasure Valley Triathlon: the first-timer's race

- **When:** Saturday, June 5, 2027
- **Where:** Esther Simplot Park, along the Boise River in Boise
- **Format:** A circuit-style triathlon on a closed course. The organizers describe it as flat, fast and good for racers of all levels.
- **Events:** Super Sprint, Sprint and Olympic, plus relays, adaptive categories and a youth Splash and Dash
- **Sanctioning:** USAT sanctioned. In 2027 it's also the USAT Idaho State Championship.

Why it's a good first race: the course is a loop, so you pass the same spots more than once, and friends and family can watch from one place. A closed course means no traffic to worry about. And with a Super Sprint and a relay on offer, there's a way in for nearly everyone, from someone trying all three sports for the first time to someone teaming up with two friends.

The race lists its swim, bike and run distances on its own course pages, so check there before you plan your training.

[Race page and registration](https://runsignup.com/Race/ID/Boise/TreasureValleyTriathlon)

## IRONMAN 70.3 Boise: the big one

- **When:** Saturday, July 24, 2027. It ran on July 25, 2026 and July 26, 2025, so it's settled into late July.
- **Where:** The swim is at Lucky Peak Reservoir. The finish is downtown.
- **Distance:** A 70.3 is 1.2 miles of swimming, 56 miles of biking and 13.1 miles of running.
- **The swim:** A reservoir swim at Lucky Peak, starting and finishing at Barclay Bay.
- **The bike:** Begins with a long, sweeping downhill out of the reservoir area, then winds through rural roads and neighborhoods, and finishes downtown.
- **The run:** Two loops of the Boise River Greenbelt, with plenty of shade and spectators, finishing downtown.

IRONMAN returned to Boise in 2025, and for local athletes there's something special about racing on roads and paths you train on. It's a big commitment, though. Most people spend months building up to a half-distance race.

[Official race page](https://www.ironman.com/races/im703-boise)

## Which one should you pick?

| If you... | Try |
|---|---|
| have never done a triathlon | Treasure Valley Triathlon, Super Sprint or Sprint |
| have done a sprint and want more | Treasure Valley Triathlon, Olympic |
| want a big challenge with a long build | IRONMAN 70.3 Boise, in a later year |
| want to try it with friends | A Treasure Valley Triathlon relay |

## How to plan it

June 5 is a little over seven months away, which is plenty of time for a first sprint. Our [8-week sprint plan](/training-plans) works backwards from race day: start around early April, and use the winter to build a base of easy swimming, riding and running.

If you're thinking about a 70.3, treat a sprint or Olympic as your stepping stone first. Do the shorter race, find out which part you love and which you don't, and build from there.
